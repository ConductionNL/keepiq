# Design: share-save no longer misses partner shares without saying so

## Context

- `ShareSaveScope::requirement()` builds what a share-save must name. On the copy-editor path it requires the source and every other copy, no partner shares, and answers `ownerOnly` when the source has any. On the owner path it requires every copy and the active partner shares of the source, filtered to those whose owner is the caller.
- `ShareSaveService::assertCopyEditorRequest()` refuses partner entries from a copy editor (`federated_owner_only`).
- A transfer (`SecretOwnerTransferService::transfer`) makes the new owner the source's owner and hands its partner shares to them, but the source stays encrypted for the previous owner. Team offboarding (`TeamSecretTransferService::transfer`) needs the successor to hold a judged recipient copy already, so the successor edits through it.
- Account deletion (`AccountDeletionService::deleteAllFor`) snapshots the user's secrets, transfers the delegated ones, and then runs `AccountShareCleanupService::detachGrantedShares` over the whole snapshot, which tombstones and unlinks every copy of a transferred secret too.
- `FederatedShareService::create` checks only that the caller owns the row and `assertOnwardShareable()`, so a recipient's full copy passes.

## Goals

- Every active partner share of a secret is written by a save of its owner, or the save says it wasn't.
- The successor of an offboarded user and the delegate of a deleted user reach the secret's partner shares with their next save.

## Non-goals

- Writing partner shares from a copy editor who doesn't own the source; partner shares stay the owner's.
- Keeping other recipients' copies linked on account deletion; only the delegate's own copy is kept.
- Repairing delegate copies that earlier account deletions already detached.
- The received-copy refusal on the team-folder fan-out, which the user-sharing spec lists as a separate follow-up.
- `PUT /api/v1/secrets/{id}`, which writes no copies and no partner shares by design.

## Decisions

**D1. The owner writes partner shares through a copy.** A copy editor whose user id is the source's owner id takes the owner's partner-share rules: `requirement()` requires the source's active partner shares and answers `ownerOnly: false`. Everything else about the copy-editor path stays, including `sourceBaseUpdatedAt`. Ownership is read again from the source row under the lock, on both paths: a caller who no longer owns the source gets the copy-editor rules, and on the owner path a caller whose secret passed to someone else meanwhile is refused as not the owner, with nothing written. A copy editor who doesn't own the source, whether a delegate, a write-grade editor or an owner whose secret passed on between two attempts, and who names partner shares (`federated` or `suspend`) gets 409 `incomplete` requiring none, on a one-call save and on every stage call, before the lock as under it. The answer echoes only the ids they sent, so they learn nothing about the partner shares; their browser drops them and saves again. This replaces the 400 `federated_owner_only`, so one rule covers every non-owner, and nobody needs telling apart by their ownership history. The clients look up and verify the certificates of the partner shares the server requires; on the copy-editor path they do so when the `incomplete` answer names partner shares, and send them with the next attempt.

**D2. A partner share that isn't the owner's stops the save.** On every path that writes partner shares (D1 included), an active partner share of the source whose owner isn't the source's owner makes share-save answer 409 `partner_share_owner_mismatch` and write nothing. The server logs a warning with the secret id and the share ids. The clients show the refusal as an error that names the cause and the remedy: an administrator runs `occ maintenance:repair`, whose `ReownTransferredPartnerShares` step hands the share to the secret's owner. Refusing, not reporting, keeps the drift visible until it is fixed, and such a share isn't served to the partner anyway.

**D3. A received copy is never a partner share's source.** `FederatedShareService::create` refuses a source that is a recipient's copy, with the test group shares use (`ShareRowJudge::isReceivedCopy`) and the refusal `register-batch` gives, `not_shareable`. There is no delegate exception: partner shares are the owner's (D1), and a delegate shares with a partner only once they own the secret. Which share rows count follows the runtime rules for judged rows; a row the pending quarantine scan cannot judge answers 409 `share_scan_pending`, so an unclear case never lets a copy out, and a row the scan quarantines doesn't make its secret a copy. The source's owner is the one exception, because D1 leaves them working through their own copy: when they share that copy, `create` follows the trusted share row to the source (`FederatedShareSources::ownedSourceOf`) and makes the partner share there, with the ownership, onward-share and scan checks run on the source. Only the row leads there, never an id from the request, and a copy of someone else's secret is refused as before. The list of partner shares for that copy follows the same mapping: it shows the source's partner shares next to those made from the copy before D3, so the owner sees and revokes the share where they made it; a holder who doesn't own the source sees only their copy's. For the same reason `already_shared` checks both: a live share to the recipient on the source or on the owner's copy refuses a second one, and the owner revokes the copy's share before sharing again.

**D4. Partner shares made from a copy before D3 are reported.** share-save counts the active partner shares whose source is a copy the save writes. For the source's owner (owner path and D1) that is every copy the save is required to write and doesn't skip, their own copy included. A caller who doesn't own the source gets the count for their own copy only: the other copies' partner shares are a fact about other holders, and the owner's partner shares reach that caller only as the `ownerOnly` flag, not as a number, so neither does another holder's. The source's own partner shares are not counted: the save writes them (owner path and D1), or `ownerOnly` already reports them. The count is answered as `federated.notReached`, and the clients say that this many recipients at partner organisations got the secret from a copy and didn't get the change. The share-save doesn't write them: they hang off a copy whose holder may not be the caller, so the caller can't be required to encrypt for them, and D3 keeps new ones from appearing. A count shows no partner, cloud id or share id.

**D5. Account deletion keeps the delegate's own copy.** `transferDelegatedSecrets` reports which secrets it handed to which delegate. For those secrets, `detachGrantedShares` leaves the share row whose target is the new owner, and the copy it links, untouched, when nothing limits the delegate's access: the runtime row judge trusts the row, the copy is not use-only, expiring or read-only, the row carries no limit of its own, and every grant reaching the copy (direct, group or team folder) is unlimited. Every other copy is detached as before, and so is the delegate's when any of these fails. Limits often live on a group share the cascade removes, so a kept limited row would end up with more access than before, or, beside a full grant that goes, with less; detaching keeps every limit exactly as it was. `DelegateCopyKeeper::keptRow` makes this choice before anything is deleted. A kept row loses its group share and team folder and stands as a direct share, since the deleted user's team-folder rows and memberships outlive her folders, and a member leaving one would otherwise remove the delegate's only readable copy. When the kept row names the deleted user as its maker, that is replaced with the deleted-account marker, as for link shares. The kept copy makes the delegate a copy editor who owns the source, so D1 applies. Group-share definitions of the secret are still removed.

## Risks and trade-offs

- D2 blocks an owner's value edit until an administrator runs the repair step. The repair step runs after every upgrade (`post-migration`), so this only happens with drift written after it last ran.
- D4 leaves partner shares made from a copy on their old value. Their partners keep that value until the copy holder revokes the share; the clients say so on every save that touches them.
- D5 keeps one share row of a deleted user's secret. A maker that was the deleted user becomes the deleted-account marker, so no personal data of the deleted user stays on it.
- D5 detaches a delegate's limited copy as before, so that delegate still ends up owning a secret they can't decrypt, and their saves reach nobody. Their copy keeps its value and its limits until they end.
